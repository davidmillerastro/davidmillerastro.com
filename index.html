Welcome!

My name is David Miller, and I am a PhD candidate in astronomy at the University of British Columbia. My doctoral research is supervised by Jeremy Heyl and was previously supervised by the late Harvey Richer. I received my MSc in Astronomy from UBC and my BSc in Honours Physics from Simon Fraser University. I expect to complete my PhD in 2027 and am currently seeking postdoctoral opportunities beginning in fall 2027.

My research uses white dwarfs in open clusters to study stellar evolution, with a particular focus on the initial-final mass relation: the connection between a star's initial mass and the white dwarf it eventually becomes. I make extensive use of data from the Gaia space telescope, along with spectroscopy and imaging from facilities including Gemini, CFHT, and the Hubble Space Telescope. I also maintain wdifmr.org, an online resource for observational measurements of the white dwarf initial-final mass relation. You can learn more about my work through the Research and Publications sections, view my complete publication record on NASA ADS , and find recent updates in Latest News below.
